**Title:** Selling in Canada: does a fixed or painted-over problem still need to be disclosed?

Something I keep seeing when people sell: they assume that if a problem is fixed, or at least not visible anymore, they don't have to mention it. In most of Canada that's not really how it works (Quebec has its own system).

Roughly, you're not obligated to point out patent defects, the stuff a buyer would catch on a normal inspection. But you generally have to disclose latent defects you actually know about. Those are hidden problems that make the place dangerous or unfit to live in. The bigger trap is the other half of the rule: you can't lie, give a half answer, or cover something up. Painting over a water stain on a basement wall before listing is the classic example. Courts tend to treat that as concealment, and that's how sellers get sued after closing for repair bills that can run well into six figures.

The other one that catches people is the disclosure form. Ticking "unknown" when you got a contractor quote on the foundation last year is not a safe answer.

If you've had a past leak, the usual advice is to disclose it and include the paperwork showing what was done.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** As a home seller in Canada, do I have to disclose a past basement leak that I already fixed?

Short answer: if you know about it and a buyer couldn't easily see it, you should disclose it. And you must never hide it.

Here's how it generally works in most of Canada outside Quebec. The old buyer-beware rule still covers patent defects, meaning problems a buyer could reasonably spot on a normal inspection. You don't have to point those out. Latent defects are different. If you know about a hidden problem that makes the home dangerous or unfit to live in, you're expected to disclose it. Whichever category it falls into, you can't lie, give a misleading half answer, or actively conceal anything.

A fixed leak can be a gray area under the first test, but it often fails the second. If the repair was really just paint or new drywall over a stain, a court can treat that as concealment. If the buyer later finds water and your old contractor quote turns up, the claim can cover repairs, remediation and legal costs. That's how a small leak becomes a very large number.

What we tell sellers:

- If the leak was properly repaired, say so and attach the invoice and any warranty. A documented fix reads as diligence, not a red flag.
- On a property disclosure form, never tick "unknown" for something you know about. Answer accurately, or ask your lawyer how to word it.
- Don't cosmetically cover evidence before listing.
- Quebec uses the Civil Code's legal warranty of quality, which is stricter, so get local advice there.
