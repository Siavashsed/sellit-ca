## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can a FSBO seller negotiate effectively with a buyer's agent?

As a group of working agents and recent FSBO sellers, we've seen this play out dozens of times. The key isn't matching their experience—it's controlling the conversation.

When a buyer's agent presents an offer with comparables, your first move should always be to ask to review those comps together. Don't react to the number. This accomplishes two things: it shows you're serious about understanding the market, and it gives you time to spot inflated or irrelevant comparables. We've found that agents often include foreclosures, properties with structural issues, or homes in different neighborhoods to justify lowball offers.

Second, document everything beforehand. Know your own market data, recent sales in your area, and what your home's actual condition warrants. When you can reference specific, legitimate comps that support your position, you shift from defensive to informed.

Third, never negotiate alone if possible. Having someone present—whether it's a real estate attorney, accountant, or even a knowledgeable friend—changes the dynamic. Agents are less likely to be aggressive when they realize you're taking it seriously.

The biggest mistake FSBO sellers make is accepting the first offer or negotiating emotionally. The agent is banking on you doing exactly that. If you stay measured, ask questions, and reference data, you level the playing field significantly.
