## QUORA
**Answer this question:** What should I do if my home appraisal comes in lower than the accepted sale price?

A low appraisal feels like a deal-killer, but it usually isn't. As a group of working agents, listing photographers, mortgage brokers and recent FSBO sellers, we see this regularly, and you generally have four options: challenge the number, split the gap with the buyer, drop the price, or walk away. Most sellers jump to the last one, and that is typically the wrong call.

Start by getting a copy of the appraisal through the buyer's lender and reading it line by line. Appraisers can use comparable sales from the wrong area, overlook renovations, or record the wrong square footage. If you find real errors, your agent can submit a reconsideration of value with three to five stronger recent comparables, plus a short list of upgrades with dates and costs. Keep it factual. Emotional arguments do not move appraisers.

If the number holds, treat the gap as a negotiation. Buyers have already paid for inspections, locked a rate and often arranged their move, so many will cover some of the difference in cash. A common outcome is to split it. You lower the price by part of the gap and the buyer brings extra down payment for the rest.

Dropping the price to the appraised value is the cleanest route if you need certainty and speed. Walking away and relisting only makes sense if you have strong evidence the market will pay more, because the next buyer's appraiser may reach a similar number, and you will have lost weeks.

Before you decide, work out what each path actually costs you in dollars and time. We wrote a longer playbook with examples here if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/sellit-ca/appraisal-gap-below-accepted-price/

In short, do not react on day one. Read the report, check the comps, and decide with numbers.
