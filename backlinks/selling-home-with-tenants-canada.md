## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Can I sell my house in Canada if it has tenants living in it?

Yes, you can. But it helps to understand what you're actually selling: the house plus the existing lease.

In most Canadian provinces, the tenant has the right to stay after the sale. The buyer takes over as landlord on the same terms, including the rent and the lease length. The tenant only has to leave if they agree to, or if the buyer qualifies to move in themselves. In that case the buyer usually has to give proper notice, and in some provinces pay compensation. Rules differ by province, so check your local tenancy board before you list.

There are a few practical points we've seen as working agents:

1. Give written notice before every showing, as your province requires. Don't assume goodwill.
2. Schedule around the tenant's life. In a Hamilton sale we helped with, showings at 6 p.m. on weekdays worked well for everyone.
3. Talk to the tenant early. A cooperative tenant makes photography, showings and buyer confidence far easier.
4. Be upfront about the lease. Give buyers a copy, the rent amount and the term. Investors may actually value a tenant already in place.
5. Consider a cash-for-keys offer if you want vacant possession. It must be voluntary, and it should be in writing.
6. Expect a different buyer pool. Owner-occupiers may hesitate, while investors may see it as a plus.

The biggest mistake is trying to hide the tenancy or pressure the tenant. It tends to backfire, with delays, disputes or a collapsed deal.
