**Title:** Is waiting for the spring rush actually worth it when you're selling? Inventory in your price band seems to matter more than the month

Every year around this time people ask whether they should list now or hold off until spring. Having watched a lot of listings, I think the season tells you less than most people assume. What matters more is how many homes are actively listed in your price band, and whether that number is going up or down.

If your home is ready and inventory in your band is flat or falling, listing now usually beats waiting. Buyers have fewer options, so yours gets more attention. If you wait for the "spring rush", you can end up launching in the busiest weeks of the year, up against every other seller who had the same idea.

I think the only good reason to wait is a specific problem you can fix: a roof that will fail inspection, a basement that needs a dry season, a renovation that's half done. Waiting to fix something real is a plan. Waiting for a date on the calendar is a guess.

A quick way to check: pull the active listing counts for your area and price range over the last 8 to 12 weeks. Look at which way the count is moving, not the count itself.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Should I sell my house now or wait until spring when more buyers are looking?

The short answer from our desk: if your house is ready to show and inventory in your price band is flat or falling, list now. Wait only if a specific problem you can fix is holding the house back.

We are a small group of working agents, listing photographers, mortgage brokers and recent FSBO sellers. The question we hear most is some version of "should I wait for spring?" It assumes spring always brings more buyers, and it often does. But spring also brings more sellers, and they are the ones competing with you for those buyers.

So the better question is not "what month is it?" It is "how many homes like mine are for sale right now, and is that number going up or down?"

Here is how we would approach it:

1. Get the active listing counts for your area and price range for the last 8 to 12 weeks. An agent can pull this in minutes, or you can track it yourself on a listing site.
2. If the count is flat or falling, buyers in your band have fewer choices. That works in your favour in any season.
3. If the count is rising quickly, price sharply or launch before the next wave of listings arrives.
4. If the house has a real problem, like a roof near the end of its life, a half-finished renovation or water in the basement, waiting to fix it first is a sound plan. Waiting only for the calendar is a guess.

We have seen sellers hold a ready house off the market for the spring rush and lose the quieter weeks when it would have stood out. We wrote up the full reasoning here if you want more detail: https://siavashsed.github.io/sellit-ca/market-timing-list-this-season-or-next/

Whatever you choose, base the decision on your local numbers, not the month.
