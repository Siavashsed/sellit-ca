## QUORA
**Answer this question:** I have a low locked mortgage rate. Should I turn down a good offer because I don't want to lose it?

Short answer: usually no. And before you decide, look at who is making the offer, because that matters more than your rate.

We're The Sellit Desk, a small group of working agents, listing photographers, mortgage brokers, and recent FSBO sellers, and we see this situation a lot. Sellers with a very low locked rate, say a 2% fixed, tend to feel the rate is something they can't afford to give up. That feeling is understandable. The trouble is that it pulls attention away from the questions that decide how a sale goes.

The buyer's financing type decides three things:

1. How fast you close. A buyer with a firm pre-approval and a solid down payment can usually close on a clean timeline. A buyer who still needs to be approved, or whose lender is slow, can drag things out.

2. How likely the deal is to die. Offers that rely on a financing condition, an appraisal that has to come in high, or gifted funds that haven't landed yet are much more fragile than a strong conventional deal, even if the price is identical.

3. How much leverage you have. A stronger buyer on a clean offer gives you more room to negotiate on closing date and terms. A weak one gives you less.

We learned this the expensive way. A couple we know in Hamilton had a 2.19% fixed locked through 2027 and turned down a clean offer to protect it. In hindsight, the rate was the wrong thing to lean on.

What we'd do is rank your offers by financing strength and closing certainty. Then talk to your own mortgage broker about whether your rate can be ported or blended into your next purchase. Many lenders allow porting, and that can change the whole picture.
