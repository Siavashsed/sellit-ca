## QUORA
**Answer this question:** How can a home seller tell if a buyer's mortgage pre-approval letter is actually reliable?

Short answer: treat the letter as a starting point, not proof. A pre-approval generally shows that a lender reviewed a buyer's credit and the income and savings numbers the buyer reported. It usually does not show that those numbers were verified, that the funds are in place, or that the property will pass the lender's review. That gap is where many good-looking offers fall apart.

Here is what we check as working agents, brokers and photographers who see lots of offers:

1. The date. A letter that is several weeks old predates rate changes and any change in the buyer's finances, such as a new car loan or job change.

2. What was verified. Ask whether the lender saw pay stubs, tax documents and bank statements, or simply took the buyer's word. Verified is much stronger.

3. The down payment. Ask for proof of funds, and look for money that has been in the account long enough to have a clear paper trail. Gifted funds need documentation too.

4. The lender. Have your agent call the named broker or bank. A real professional will confirm the status in a few minutes. If you can't reach anyone, that's useful information.

5. The conditions. Look at what the offer depends on: financing, appraisal, property approval. More conditions mean more places for the deal to slip.

6. The gap between letter and price. A letter for more than the offer isn't proof of anything on its own.
