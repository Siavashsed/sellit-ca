**Title:** 41 groups came to an open house and nobody made an offer. Has anyone else found that traffic means nothing without a deadline?

Something we keep seeing with FSBO sellers: a packed open house means very little on its own. One seller we worked with had a semi in Hamilton's Crown Point. Her first Saturday open house ran 1 to 3 pm and brought 41 groups through. She got zero offers, not even a lowball.

Looking back, the house and the price weren't the problem. Nothing pushed anyone to decide. There was no deadline, the sign-in sheet collected little more than first names, and there was no plan to follow up. People left thinking "nice place" and went to the next listing.

Three unexciting moves fixed it:
1. Announce an offer date up front so buyers know there's a time limit.
2. Use a sign-in sheet that asks about pre-approval and whether they have an agent, so you can tell real buyers from browsers.
3. Call every serious group within 24 hours, while the house is still fresh in their minds.

Cookies and candles made no difference at all.

## QUORA
**Answer this question:** Why do open houses get lots of visitors but no offers?

We see this all the time, and it rarely comes down to the house itself. A busy open house and an open house that produces offers are two different things. The difference is structure.

Three things are usually missing:

1. A deadline. If buyers think the house will still be available next week, most of them will wait. Announce an offer date before the open house and repeat it at the door. That turns casual browsing into a decision with a time limit. Even motivated buyers put things off when there's no deadline.

2. A qualified sign-in sheet. A list of first names and emails tells you nothing. Ask whether they're pre-approved, whether they have an agent, and when they hope to move. That separates the neighbours and weekend browsers from the two or three groups who could actually write an offer.

3. A follow-up call within 24 hours. Buyers often see five or six houses in a weekend, and by Monday they blur together. A short call the next day keeps your listing in the running. Ask what they thought and whether anything is holding them back, and you'll hear objections you can actually address.

What matters much less: cookies, scented candles and background music. They make the visit pleasant, but they don't create any pressure to decide.

One of the FSBO sellers in our group learned this with a semi in Hamilton's Crown Point. Her first Saturday open house brought 41 groups through and produced zero offers. The next weekend she rebuilt it around a deadline and real follow-up. If you're getting plenty of visitors but no offers, fix the process before you touch the price.
